Introduction  \nThis document defines the physical security controls to be implemented for location s \nhousing SCADA and RTU equipment to prevent sabotaging of these equipment.  \n \n1.1 Operational Technology (OT)  Physical Security and Facility Types  \nThe need for Physical Security and Facilities Management shall be applied in the following \nareas in OT environment:  \na. Physical security perimeters ; \nb. Physical access systems ;  \nc. Visitor access management ; \nd. Facilities management ; and   \ne. Protection of Critical Information Infrastructure ( CII) assets in substations \nagainst unauthorised access . \n \n1.2 Glossary  \nThis document will consistently use the following technical terms to describe core \ninformation security concepts and artefacts. These technical terms are  commonly used in \ninternational standards and best practices documents as well.  \n \nField device:  \nHardware and software system that monitor and control physical devices in the field. For \nexample, Programmable Logic Controller (PLC) and Distributed Control Sys tem (DCS).  \n \nRemote Terminal Unit (RTU) : \nAn electronic device that is controlled by a microprocessor.  \n \nSupervisory control and data acquisition (SCADA):  \nA system of software and hardware element that allows industrial organisations to control \nindustrial processes locally or at remote location; monitor, gather, and process real -time \ndata.  \n \n2. Physical Security Protection  Requirements  \n2.1 Physical security documentation  \n1. Floor layout shall be documented and maintained  for the area housing the SCADA \nequip ment . \n \n2. Closed circuit television ( CCTV ) layout shall be documented and maintained.  \n \n3. Service Level Agreement arrangements shall be in place to maintain the quality of \nphysical security systems and security manpower providers . \n ', '  SP Group  \nVersion 1. 2  Page 6 Internal  2.2 Physical security architecture  \n1. The physical area supporting OT operations shall be categorised into three types:  \na. SCADA Control – this physical zone refers to the area where the front -end \nprocessors, network infrastructure , and SCADA workstations (e.g. operator \nworkstations, admin workstations) are housed ; \nb. Substation – this physical zone refers to the area wher e RTU, network equipment, \nand condition monitoring equipment are housed , together  with other \nequipment ; and  \nc. RTU box – this physical zone refers to area when RTU are housed outdoor.  \n \n2.3 Physical security implementation for SCADA control area  \n1. If SCADA control a rea is located at the low floors and has window openings to the \nexternal façade of the building, the window opening shall  be protected . \n \n2. Access into the SCADA Control area shall be access controlled using card \nauthentication mechanism or equivalent.  \n \n3. A SCADA Control area may house another SCADA Control area with potential further \naccess control between the rooms , depending on the personnel authorised to access \nthese areas. For example, if there is an adjacent SCADA Control area where all \npersonnel in Room A can enter Room B, there will not be a need for access control in \nbetween the rooms. However, if not all personnel in Room A can enter Room B, then \nthere is a need for access control in between the rooms.  \n \n4. Doors leading into SCADA Control area shall be kept in closed position at all times to \nminimise the risk of unauthorised access.  \n \n5. All door access control devices (e.g. card readers, biometrics readers) shall be \napproved by the  Corporate  Security Branch.  \n \n6. Recording of continuous CCTV footage shall be retained based on the security \nassessments and requirements . \n \n7. Keys for securing equipment and area shall be kept in a safe and accountable \nenvironment.  \n \n2.4 Physical Security Implementation for substation  area  \n1. The substation shall be protected by  multi layer defence such as  pad lock s and Fence  \nIntrusion Detection System ( FIDS), etc . \n \n2. Access doors shall be locked when unoccupied.  \n \n3. All physical door locks shall be approved by the Corporate  Security Branch.  \n \n4. CCTV cameras shall  be installed at all main entry and exit of the transmission \nsubstations to capture the in/out of the area.  ', '  SP Group  \nVersion 1. 2  Page 7 Internal   \n5. CCTV footage for transmission substations shall be recorded for a minimum period of \n90 days.  \n \n6. Keys for securing area shall be kept in a safe and accountable environment. , where \napplicable . \n \n2.5 Physical security implementation for RTU boxes  \n1. Outdoor e nclosure housing for RTU shall  be installed in the line of sight of public \nsecurity CCTV cameras, where possible.  \n \n2. The outdoor enclosure housing  used  for Elect  and Gas RTU shall be minimally \nProtections rated  IP55 for Ingress Protection . \n \n3. Door of RTU enclosure shall be locked when unoccupied.  \n \n4. Enclosure door locks used shall be approved by the SCADA team.  \n \n3. Physical Security Operations Requirements  \n3.1 Physical access management  for SCADA control area  \n1. The granting of physical access into SCADA control area shall be approved by business \nowner and administered by the  IAM team,  based on access control matrix. Access \nrights of identification pass shall be reviewed and updated on an annual basis.  \n \n2. Physical access log shall be reviewed when there are reported or observed s uspicious \nactivities and once a year to identify possible security threats and incidents.  \n \n3. Process shall be in place to  ensure the removal of access rights  when a staff leaves \nthe organisation . \n \n4. Lost access card shall be reported to  Corporate Security Branch  as soon as the loss is \ndiscovered  and the access rights of the card shall be revoked as practicable as \npossible.  \n \n3.2 Physical access management for substations and RTU boxes  \n1. A list of authorised personnel who can access substation area and RTU boxes shall be \nmaintained.  \n \n2. Door keys for substations are kept in a safe and accountable environment  and \nmaintained by individual section . Key shall be drawn and returned for each physic al \naccess request. A record of key movement shall be maintained.  \n ', '  SP Group  \nVersion 1. 2  Page 8 Internal  3.3 Physical access management for field devices  \n1. Field devices shall be protected in locked enclosures, where feasible. If field devices \ncannot be physically protected, they shall be logically protected through enabling \nauthentication with  READ only access . \n \n2. Keys must be kept in a safe and accountable en vironment  \n \n3.4 Managing visitors and contractors  \n1. Logs/records of a ll suppliers and contractors  entering  the substation shall be kept . \n \n2. Suppliers and contractors shall be escorted when accessing SCADA control area, \nsubstation area, as well as accessing configur ation items within the RTU enclosure.  \n \n4. Physical Security Maintenance Requirements   \n4.1 Handling of renovations  \n1. All renovations to be carried out, shall identify the possible security concerns during \nand after renovation period. Security concern includes but not limited to the closure \nof publicly accessible areas (e.g. toilets), contractor access, change of use of the area, \nand physical access changes.  \n \n4.2 Substation security maintenance  \n1. Locks to the substation shall be checked periodically  for visib le signs of tampering \nand/or corrosion.  \n \n2. Keys to the locks shall be tested to ensure that they can be used to open the locks.  \n \n4.3 RTU box security maintenance  \n1. The RTU boxes shall be checked at least once every three years to ensure that there \nis no water seep age and corroded RTU components.  \n \n2. Locks to the RTU door enclosures shall be checked every three years for visible signs \nof tampering and/or corrosion.  \n \n4.4 CCTV maintenance  \n1. The quality of CCTV images shall be checked on every guard shift to  ensure that it \nmaintains its clarity for evidence admissibility.  \n ', '  SP Group  \nVersion 1. 2  Page 9 Internal  5. Responding to Physical Security Violations Requirements  \n5.1 Detection of unauthorised access into substation and RTU boxes  \n1. In the event when the physical security for substation or RTU boxes suspected to be \ncompromised, technical officers shall be despatch ed immediately to investigate the \nsituation.  \n \n2. In the event where visible signs of lock tampering detected , the officer shall r eport as \na security event for further investigation.  \n \n5.2 Detection of unauthorised access into SCADA control area  \n1. Process shall be in place to call the Security Command Centre and the Singapore \nPolice  Force for assistance when the security guard detects an unauthorised physical \nintrusion into SCADA Control area . \n \n6. Facilities Management Requirements  \n6.1 Facilities management documentation  \n1. Electrical single line diagram shall be documented and maintained for all SCADA \nequipment including RTU.  \n \n2. Fire escape routes and the location of firefighting equipment shall be documented \nand displayed.  \n \n6.2 Facilities management zones  \n1. There are two different facilities management zones:  \na. SCADA – this facilities management zone is used to support demanding power \nand coo ling requirements  for SCADA and network equipment. Any disruption to \nthis zone may potentially cause total loss of monitoring and control over \nelectricity and gas supply. For example, control room and process area ; and  \nb. RTU – this facilities management zone is used to suppor t RTU equipment where \nRTU is expected to continue function for a period  of time  until power is resumed. \nFor example, substation and RTU boxes.  \n \n2. There are no dependencies between the different facilities management zones.  \n \n6.3 Facilities implementation for SCAD A environment  \n1. Incoming power feeds for main control centre shall come from two different \nsubstations.  \n \n2. Three power sources  shall be provisioned within the high demand zone: Power \nsource A, Power source B, and auxiliary power (provided by the building).  ', '  SP Group  \nVersion 1. 2  Page 10 Internal   \n3. Primary distribution units (PDU) with independent circuit breakers shall be installed \nto allow electrical issues to be local ised.  \n \n4. Uninterruptible power supply (UPS) with N+1 configuration shall be used for power \ncleaning, as well as keeping the system and network devices running for a short \nperiod of time. Emergency power switch shall be installed to disconnect the power \nsourc e during emergency situations.  \n \n5. Automatic transfer switch (ATS) or static transfer switch (STS) shall be used to allow \nswitching from one load to another load.  \n \n6. Diesel generator with N+1 configuration shall be installed as a power source for \nprolonged peri od of power disruption.  \n \n7. Employee, contractors, and visitors are prohibited from plugging their own power \nstrips into any of the power source provided in the SCADA environment . \n \n8. Emergency lighting shall be installed to illuminate the area during power outage.  \n \n9. Computer room air conditioning (CRAC) shall be installed with a minimum N+1 \nconfiguration to provide sufficient cooling.  \n \n10. Water detection leakage sensors shall be placed along water prone areas (e.g. \nrainwater  downpipes, near proximity to wash are as, as well as CRAC units) to detect \npotential water leakages that may create power outages.  \n \n11. Humidifiers shall be installed with a minimum of N+1 configuration to regulate the \nmoisture in the room.  \n \n12. Water chiller on N+1 configuration shall be installed to ensure sufficient cooling \nneeds are met.  \n \n13. Industrial fans or standby air -con system shall be available for emergency purposes \nto cool the area in the event where cooling system fails.  \n \n14. Network cables used for wiring shall be electromagneticall y shielded (e.g. CAT6).  \n \n15. Power and network cables shall be physically separated to reduce electronic \nmagnetic interference (EMI).  \n \n16. Network cables shall be labelled with a documented code for easy maintenance.  \n \n17. Network cables shall run on cable trays to pre vent cable breakage.  \n \n18. Unsecured cabling across aisles or on the floor is strictly prohibited. All devices shall \nbe installed in racks or cabinets.  \n \n19. Fire extinguishers shall be installed at designated spots within the area.  \n ', '  SP Group  \nVersion 1. 2  Page 11 Internal  20. Gas-based fire suppression syste m and dry pipe water sprinklers shall be installed for \nfire suppression. Fire suppression abort button shall be installed to allow emergency \nabortion of gas release action.  \n \n21. Smoke detectors shall be installed within the area.  \n \n6.4 Facilities implementation for RTU environment  \n1. Substation or RTU charger shall be used to allow RTU to continue functioning for a \nperiod of time.  \n \n2. Access doors shall have the same fire rating as the wall it is constructed on . \n \n7. Facilities Operations  Requirements  \n7.1 Power equipment management in the SCADA environment  \n1. Electrical loading shall be reviewed before new equipment is added into the \nenvironment for SCADA environment . \n \n2. UPS loading shall be maintained below 40% of the master -slave module  so that there \nwill be sufficient power for failover situations.  \n \n3. Power generator shall be tested on monthly basis and left running for a minimum \nperiod of 30 minutes.  \n \n7.2 Cooling equipment management in the SCADA environment  \n1. The optimal operating temperature and humidity in in SCADA environment  shall be \nmaintained within the range of the equipment operating specifications.  \n \n7.3 Fire prevention management for SCADA environment  \n1. Fire prevention program shall be in place, with an appo inted fire prevention officer \nto coordinate the implementation of the program. All staff shall  be informed of their \nrespective role in the program and appropriate training to be given where necessary.  \n \n2. Combustible materials (e.g. wastepaper , toner fluid, gas cylinders) shall  not be stored \nin SCADA environment . \n \n3. Local regulations as to the minimum set of equipment to be provided shall be \nobserved.  \n \n4. All escape routes shall  be kept clear to ensure they are passable in low visibility or \npanic si tuations.  \n \n5. The fire doors shall  not be locked or blocked.  \n ', '  SP Group  \nVersion 1. 2  Page 12 Internal  8. Facilities Management Maintenance Requirements   \n8.1 Power equipment maintenance  \n1. Safety checks on electrical installations shall be conducted by specialists at least once \na year.  \n \n2. Thermal thermometer shall be used to check for the need of tightening power \nconnection points (e.g. ATS or STS) on a yearly basis.  \n \n3. Battery strings for UPS shall be checked at least once on a quarterly basis to ensure \nthat the batteries are in good conditi ons.  \n \n8.2 Cooling equipment maintenance  \n1. CRAC or air -conditioning units shall be serviced on a quarterly basis to ensure \ncontinued operability of the machine.  \n \n2. Water chillers shall be maintained on a quarterly basis.  \n \n8.3 Fire equipment maintenance  \n1. The serviceability of emergency lighting shall be maintained on an annual basis.  \n \n2. Firefighting equipment (e.g. fire extinguisher) shall be maintained on a yearly basis.  \n \n3. The serviceability of smoke detection and fire suppression equipment shall be \nverified minimally on a quarterly basis for SCADA environment.  \n \n9. Exceptions  \n1. Any deviations from the standards shall require approval from the SP Group Chief \nInformation  Officer  or delegate . \n \n10.
